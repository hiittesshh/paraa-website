## Recommendation: pursue **A, "The Open Book"**, as the spine, and graft in three parts

**Why A wins.** The old site's core trust problem is numbers nobody can check ("680% growth since
inception", "India's #1", an empty "Registered with:"). A answers that problem directly: every
figure is dated, sourced, revisable and downloadable. It tied D for the highest
distinctiveness score. Its trust also depends on the one thing Paraa already produces (a NAV per
basket), not on licences or people that aren't confirmed yet.

**What to graft in:**
1. **From D:** the `/risks` page, "closure conditions written in advance", and the loss simulator
   as a homepage section, not the hero. This makes A's honesty two-sided.
2. **From B:** the birth-year calculator as the `/learn` entry point and a shareable tool. It's the
   best top-of-funnel idea, and it makes the "dollar appreciation" pitch honest by showing
   the years the rupee won.
3. **From C:** the `/advisors` page and the "say this, not that" card for partners, and later the
   people register once names and consent are confirmed.

**Why not the others as the spine:**
- **C** depends most on facts we don't have (registrations, named people, consent to publish
  holdings). Launched today, it would be mostly `TO CONFIRM` tags.
- **B** is a great tool but a thin company story. It also changes to a light theme, while the
  screenshot's tone is dark.
- **D** is the most honest, but opening on a loss is a hard sell for a brand launch. It works
  better as a strong page than as the front door.

**What this choice gives up (the trade-off):**
- **Ongoing work.** A only works if the numbers are kept up: a NAV update, a changelog entry and an incident-log entry on time, every month. A transparency site with stale numbers is worse than an ordinary site.
- **Slower sign-ups.** "See every number first" is a slower route to app downloads than a download button would be.

## Before any concept goes live, these must be answered
These questions come from the research brief and the audit, and they block real copy for **all four** concepts:
1. **The legal route** for investor money: LRS, GIFT City/IFSC, or onshore. This decides what the site can legally say about "forex" and USD.
2. **Which entity holds which registration** (SEBI IA/PMS/AIF, IFSCA, FIU-IND), so the placeholders can be filled or removed.
3. **NAV in USD or USDT?** A public note suggests the old fund's NAV started at "$10.00 USDT", which would contradict "dollars, not USDT".
4. **Auditor and custodian** names, and whether NAV history can be published.
5. **Fee schedule and withdrawal timeline** (used in A, B and D's calculators).
6. **Any crypto remaining?** If yes, the ASCI VDA disclaimer is mandatory wherever it's mentioned.
