Chapter 18 min

Why the rupee moves

An exchange rate is a price: how many rupees one US dollar costs. Like any price, it moves when more people want one side than the other.

Exchange rates move with inflation gaps, interest rates, trade and capital flows. Here's each one in a sentence.

  • Inflation gaps: if prices rise faster in India than in the US, the rupee tends to buy fewer dollars over time; a smaller gap eases that pull.
  • Interest rates: higher rates here can draw money in and lift the rupee; lower rates can do the reverse.
  • Trade: buying more from abroad than we sell needs more dollars and can weaken the rupee; strong exports can strengthen it.
  • Capital flows: when foreign investors bring money in, the rupee can strengthen; when they take it out, it can weaken.
Quick check: prices in India rise faster than in the US for ten years. Where does that usually push the rupee?

Chapter 36 min

LRS in plain words

LRS (the Liberalised Remittance Scheme) lets each resident send up to US$250,000 abroad per financial year, across all purposes: study, travel, gifts and investments together. VERIFY

Chapter 45 min

TCS: what it is, why it isn't a fee

TCS (tax collected at source) is tax your bank collects when you send money abroad. It counts towards your income tax for the year, so it isn't a fee kept by anyone. VERIFY

When dollars are the wrong answer
