Both directions

Mostly one way. Not always.

Since 2006, the rupee has mostly weakened against the dollar. But it strengthened in 2007, 2010, 2017 and 2021. If you'd needed rupees in one of those years, dollars bought fewer of them.

When it helps: if you invest ₹1,00,000 and the rupee weakens 5%, your holding is worth about ₹1,05,000 before any investment gain or loss.

When it hurts: if the rupee strengthens 5%, the same holding is worth about ₹95,000, even if the investment didn't move.

Dollars are a tool, not a shortcut

What they can do

  • Spread your savings across two currencies, not one
  • Match future costs you'll pay in dollars (study abroad, travel, imported goods)
  • Keep their value in dollar terms when the rupee falls

What they can't do

  • Make a bad investment good
  • Help if the rupee strengthens while you need rupees
  • Remove market risk. The investment itself can still fall.

Questions people ask after running the calculator

So the rupee will keep falling?

Maybe, maybe not. It fell over most decades and rose in some years. Nobody can tell you which year comes next, and you should doubt anyone who says they can.

Is a weaker rupee good for me?

Only for money you hold in dollars. For your rupee salary and rupee spending, it usually makes imports and travel cost more.
