# Homepage audit and redesign: 10 Nov 2026

**Branch:** `feat/trust-redesign`. **Scope:** the whole homepage (the site's only page), its components and its styling.
**Your decisions, 10 Nov:** a light page with one dark band for security, and a static hero with an example app panel instead of the scenery flight. Both reverse earlier rounds (12 to 14) on purpose.

How this was checked: every file in `src/` was read, and the running page was screenshotted at 1440×900 and 390×844 with headless Chrome. The production build was measured before any change.

---

## Phase 1: what was wrong

Severity: **S1** damages trust or blocks use, **S2** a clear quality problem, **S3** polish.

### Trust and credibility

| # | Sev | Finding | Why it matters to a first-time visitor |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | S1 | The hero is a drawn sunset valley with a glowing gold sky, mist and a cartoon desk and chair. | It reads as an illustration template or generated art, not a financial company. It says nothing about the product, and it uses the most "generated" visual language on the page: gradients, glow, decoration. |
| T2 | S1 | On a 1440px screen, the first 4,200px of scrolling is one pinned animation. The basket, the facts and the ways to verify us only start after that. | A skeptic who scrolls to look for substance meets a long stretch where the page seems stuck. Mercury can afford this; an unknown fintech can't. |
| T3 | S1 | The hero asks for a mobile number before saying what happens to it. | Asking for a phone number in the first view, before anything has earned it, is a known lead-capture pattern. It lowers trust, and no privacy notice sits beside it. |
| T4 | S1 | Security facts are scattered across five places: the hero, a card grid, the carousel, the FAQ and the footer. "Our rules" mixes security with pricing. | A visitor asking "is this safe to use?" has no single place that answers it. |
| T5 | S2 | Gold is everywhere: the hero sky, the CTA band, buttons, carousel cards, highlighter strokes under four headings, slider fills and chips. | When the accent is everywhere it stops meaning anything, and the page reads as promotional rather than measured. |
| T6 | S2 | "What you get with Paraa" copies Wise's carousel: an 80px uppercase heading and giant yellow and black cards. It calls itself "the short version of everything above". | It's off the type system (80px, 28px and 72px icon circles are all outside the tokens) and repeats earlier sections. It reads as borrowed. |
| T7 | S2 | The CTA band is solid gold from edge to edge. | It reads as a sales banner. The form should feel calm and private, not loud. |
| T8 | S3 | Three almost identical app mock-ups: the SVG one in the laptop, `AppDesktop` and `AppWindow`. | The same information three times. Repetition is a generated-site tell. |

### Frontend implementation

| # | Sev | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | S1 | **266 KB of gzipped JavaScript (845 KB raw)** on a page that's mostly text. Motion drives the flight, sliders, form and art; NumberFlow drives the rolling digits. |
| F2 | S1 | **Slider lag.** The slider's fill and thumb follow the input through a spring, so the thumb trails the pointer while dragging. On a money tool, a lagging control feels broken. |
| F3 | S2 | **Theme hacks.** The `:root` palette is dark, and light cards opt out with `surface-light`, which redefines 14 variables inline. Plus raw hex in `surface-ink`, a raw `shadow-[0_24px_80px…]`, `rounded-[28px]`, `text-[48px]`/`[72px]`/`[80px]` and `size-[78px]`. All of them bypass the tokens. |
| F4 | S2 | **Duplicated components:** three app mock-ups, two "list + window" layouts (hero and tour), and two near-identical chip styles in the calculators. |
| F5 | S2 | **Heavy, fragile hero:** about 600 lines across `hero.tsx` and `hero-scene.tsx`. It measures the viewport on every resize, keeps 8 motion values, and mounts 9 full-screen SVG layers with `will-change`. That's expensive on low-end Android phones, India's most common device. |
| F6 | S3 | **The carousel's** swipe and transform logic is custom, with `aria-roledescription="carousel"` but no live region and no keyboard arrows. |
| F7 | S3 | **Dead code left behind:** `scene-drift`, `scroll-line`, `no-scrollbar` and `land-tint` CSS, and `InkTile`, `InnerPanel`, `SideHead` and `ArrowLink`, none of them used. |

What already worked and is kept: the reveal system (CSS, never hides content without JS), native `<details>` for the FAQ, the form's validation and API route, the honeypot, the `format.ts` rules, the 44px targets, the skip link, the todo guard and every tested `lib/` module.

## Phase 2: design direction

**One sentence:** a calm, white financial page where the product and the facts carry the weight, with one dark band where we talk about security, and gold kept for a few things that matter.

Reference principles, not copies:

| From | Principle we take | How it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Groww | Plain white canvas, one strong product visual, short sentences, generous spacing | White hero with an example app panel; ledes under 20 words |
| Blockaid | Security gets its own dark, technical section with facts, not adjectives | One ink band: "How we protect your account" |
| TRM Labs | Institutional structure: hairline grids, clear labels, numbers set in tabular figures, no decoration | Facts strip, hairline lists instead of cards, numbered steps on a rule |

### Tokens

- **Surfaces:** white `#FFFFFF` and grey-50 `#F8F9FD` alternate. One `surface-ink` band (`#272A33`).
- **Text:** grey-900 headings, grey-600 body, grey-500 captions (4.76:1 on white).
- **Primary action:** ink `#272A33` with white text (14.3:1). Gold is no longer a button colour.
- **Gold `#FDC037`:** the logo, the gold sleeve in the basket, the active marker in the app tour, and the slider thumb. Gold-800 `#8E6414` is the only gold text colour.
- **Corners:** 8px for buttons, inputs and chips; 12px for cards and panels; 16px for the app frame. No pills except status tags.
- **Shadow:** one, only on the app panel, because it sits over the page like a window.
- **Type:** Manrope only. Display headings at weight 600 with tight tracking, which reads more measured than 700. Same sizes as before, with the 48px cap.
- **Motion:** the existing 12px reveal, once. 150ms colour changes on things you can click. 200ms crossfade between app screens. Nothing pinned, nothing looping, no rolling digits.

### Page order

| # | Section | The question it answers | Surface |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero, with app panel | What is this, and what would I see? | White |
| 2 | Facts strip | Can I check them? | White, hairlines |
| 3 | Basket | What am I buying? | Grey-50 |
| 4 | How it works | How hard is it? | White |
| 5 | Run the numbers (two tools in one section, tabbed) | What could happen to my money? What tax would I pay? | Grey-50 |
| 6 | Security | Is my account protected? How do I spot a fake? | **Ink** |
| 7 | FAQ | What's left? | White |
| 8 | Ask a CA | Can I talk to someone first? | Grey-50 |
| 9 | Footer and legal | Who's responsible? | White |

**Removed:** the scenery flight, the pinned tour, the Wise carousel, the gold CTA band, the highlighter strokes, the animated basket drawings, and the hero phone field.

## Phase 3: priority

1. **S1 trust and performance:** replace the hero (T1–T3, F1, F5), build the security band (T4), and remove Motion and NumberFlow (F1, F2).
2. **S2 coherence:** a light token set with no inline overrides (F3), then rebuild Basket, How it works and Tools, the FAQ, the form and the footer on the same primitives (D1–D6, F4).
3. **S3:** the nav labels and dead code (D7, F7).

Not changed, because they need facts rather than design: fees, who holds the money, company details, grievance officer, legal pages. These remain launch blockers (`proposal.md` section 9). The copy keeps every limit the content audit set ("not approval of this investment", "may be paid by us", maturity wording, both directions of the rupee).

## Results (11 Nov 2026, production build)

The plan above was superseded by the staged rebuild: research, then three designers, then three developers, then a joint review. The binding spec is `design/spec.md` v1.5. These results were measured on the final production build.

| Measure | Before (10 Nov) | After | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage JS, gzip | 266 KB (845 KB raw) | **196.8 KB** (637 KB raw); our own code ≈ 27 KB | ≤ 200 / ≤ 30 |
| Page height at 1440 | 12,389 px | **7,683 px** | ≤ 7,700 |
| Page height at 390 | 15,456 px | **11,968 px** | ≤ 12,100 |
| axe (WCAG 2.2 AA + best practice), 320–1440 | not run | **0 violations** at all 6 widths | 0 |
| Lighthouse mobile, simulated (median of 3) | not run | Performance **96**, Accessibility **100**, Best practices **100**, SEO **100**; CLS 0, TBT 105 ms | LCP ≤ 2.0 s |
| LCP | | Simulated **2.6 s** (misses the budget). Observed under 4× CPU and slow 4G: the h1 paints once, at FCP (1.35 s) | |
| Sideways scroll, console errors | | none at 320/390/768/1024/1366/1440 | none |
| Placeholder or TODO text served | | 0 | 0 |

**The open LCP question.** Lighthouse's simulated model puts the h1 at 2.6 s, but every real measurement shows it painting at first paint. Blocking the web fonts doesn't change the simulated figure, so fonts are not the cause. The likely cause is that the model makes the h1 depend on scripts that finished early on localhost. Measure LCP again on the deployed site, on a real phone or with field data, before calling this closed.
